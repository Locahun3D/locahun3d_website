Which Blender 3DGS workflow? Image quality, render time and motion tests

Three full-scene cases: still image quality, processing cost and 360-degree motion.

  2. Conclusion: configuration comparison, not an overall product ranking
  3. Tested configurations and hardware

01 What this test answers

I am Kou from Locahun3D. This test imports the complete Shibuya crossing 3DGS into Blender for background footage. Import success alone is not enough: we examine the resulting image, waiting time and appearance in motion in three separate cases.

02 Conclusion: configuration comparison, not an overall product ranking

No overall winner is established. The stills and measurements below are verified for their stated configurations, but internal color processing, backgrounds and retained data differ. All four sequences and four matched-view-setting directions are now included. Internal color processing, retained data and local patches differ, so results compare stated configurations, not stock-product rankings.

04 CASE 01 | Stills from the same camera

Settings

KIRI renders all-splat proxies. BlendSplat is tested at 64 and 512 samples; SplatBake uses Bake Discs with EEVEE at 64 samples. The camera is fixed at the crossing center. Open each image at its original 1280×720 resolution.

Rendered images

Findings

Compare surface grain on the road and facades between BlendSplat 64 and 512. More samples do not reconstruct missing scan geometry. KIRI also retains glass/capture defects, moving-person remnants and unobserved regions. A successful still is not proof of fault-free motion.

In these images, BlendSplat 512 shows less fine grain around signs and facades than 64. KIRI resolves sign lettering and window frames clearly, while person remnants and black missing regions remain. SplatBake 64 renders quickly but shows surface grain.

Differences that must remain separate

A background pixel in the KIRI 90-degree image is RGBA=(0,0,0,0), i.e. transparent. The corresponding BlendSplat and SplatBake pixels are opaque gray. Black-versus-gray background is not counted as a quality win or failure. Matching view settings does not standardize internal color processing or background compositing. Standard stills below use a common gray page backdrop, without modifying the original rendered PNGs.

Four directions with matched view settings

All four configurations use Standard, exposure 0, gamma 1 and sRGB display, rendering the same camera at 0/90/180/270 degrees. This matches view settings, not necessarily internal color conversion or blending.

Findings

Lower values mean less time or memory. Read them alongside the sample count and images. RAM is the highest process sample at two-second intervals. Retained editing data differs: KIRI uses a render-only proxy setup. This is not a product-wide memory-efficiency ranking.

SplatBake had the lowest median here at 4.65s. Increasing BlendSplat from 64 to 512 samples cost approximately 1.67× the time. KIRI took about 11.60s per still and was not the fastest. These measurements alone cannot establish an overall quality-and-speed ranking.

06 CASE 03 | Motion quality in a 360-degree sequence

Settings

All four configurations use the complete scene, the same camera position, 24mm, 1280×720, 24fps and 240 frames for a stationary 360-degree turn. Videos retain their native scene view settings, without AI repair or interpolation. Use CASE 01 Standard stills for matched view settings.

Rendered sequences

Processing time

KIRI: mean 7.752s/frame, total 1865.48s. BlendSplat 512: mean 18.373s, total 4409.57s. SplatBake: mean 4.437s, total 1064.94s. The existing BlendSplat 64 video was rendered in separate batches, so no equivalently scoped total is given. Runs occurred at different times and are not an equal-quality speed ranking.

Interpreting the results

All four configurations produced 240 frames. Completion does not mean zero shimmer: compare windows, signs and road surfaces in matching directions. Reduced still grain at 512 samples does not remove missing geometry or moving-person remnants. Background black versus gray is not a quality score. Matched UE5 and equal-perceptual-quality speed tests remain unperformed. 24fps is playback speed, not interactive FPS.

07 Final assessment: what to check before adoption

The KIRI, BlendSplat and SplatBake footage and stills can now be compared; no overall stock-release recommendation is made. A full-scene workflow must succeed through output and retain the required views, not merely import.

KIRI ran in isolation with local GPU-upload and index-precision fixes. The installed addon remains unchanged. This is not a general editing/deformation tutorial.

Candidates outside this comparison

ReshotAI, Fast Splatting, Octane, Splat Forge and Splat-enabled Blender lack completed matched full-scene tests. 3DGS Painter and generation/training tools serve other purposes. Untested options receive no invented scores.

For AI finishing, see the UE5 / XGRIDs / AI test.
