Robot Development in a 3DGS City
with Isaac Sim 6.0:
RTX LiDAR, Sun Shadows, Autonomous Navigation

This is Nakamura from LOCAHUN 3D. On top of the two-layer environment from last time (untouched 3DGS + raw scanner mesh), this article runs the three things robot development actually needs in NVIDIA Isaac Sim 6.0: a real sensor (RTX LiDAR) sweeping the city, shadows that move with the time of day, and a map-based autonomous drive to a goal. The robot is Isaac's stock Nova Carter; the stage is a 16.5-million-gaussian scan of Tokyo's Shibuya scramble crossing. Videos first, then every trap you will hit if you copy this.

What you getStarting from last article's two-layer USD (3DGS + invisible collider), you end with three working pieces: (1) a stock Isaac robot driving with RTX LiDAR point-cloud capture, (2) time-of-day shadows using the raw scanner mesh as caster, (3) an occupancy grid generated from the collider and a robot following an A* path to a goal. On the simulation side everything uses extensions bundled with Isaac Sim 6.0.1 — no extra installs, no ROS 2. The three-pass shadow composite, colour grading and the omap validation are done outside Isaac (Python + NumPy/SciPy/Pillow, ffmpeg).

01 Setup and the one trap that shapes everything

The environment is unchanged from last time: appearance = untouched 3DGS (ParticleField), physics = invisible collider made of the raw scanner mesh + ground patches. Isaac Sim 6.0 moved a lot around 3DGS: import is now organised around USD ParticleField (on the NuRec (usdz) route, an NVIDIA forum answer citing the 3DGRUT docs says it is on the deprecation track with ParticleField recommended; we followed that), RTX LiDAR now lives in isaacsim.sensors.experimental.rtx, and the 6.0 docs describe a Jazzy + Pixi ROS 2 workspace for Windows (not tested in this article; feature parity with Linux unverified). All three demos here are plain Python against bundled extensions.

Test environment (what the numbers assume)Isaac Sim 6.0.1 on Windows 11 / GeForce RTX 5090 32 GB, driver 610.47; about 14 GB of VRAM with this environment loaded (16.5 M gaussians + collider + Nova Carter) / PhysX defaults (dt = 1/60, no physics material assigned = default friction, collider = triangle mesh with no approximation, 628 k triangles) / RTX render mode RaytracedLighting at 2560 × 1440 / Z-up, metersPerUnit = 1.0, world origin = the scanner's (LCC2) local origin, no geo-reference. Not tested on Linux. Capture ran at about 1.6 fps with the point cloud drawn; that cost is the Python tuple arrays handed to debug_draw, not physics or sensors. Each rendered frame advances sim time by about 1/30 s (two 60 Hz physics steps, measured from the chassis trace), so the real-time factor is about 0.05. The videos in this article play every second frame at 30 fps, i.e. about 2× sim time; all speeds and times in the text are in sim time. Note that isaacsim.sensors.experimental.rtx and isaacsim.robot.experimental.wheeled_robots are experimental namespaces whose module paths and arguments may change within 6.0.x — pin your version.

One trap governs the whole design: RTX sensors (LiDAR) do not see prims you made invisible. With the collider hidden by MakeInvisible(), the lidar passes straight through it and only hits splats, so ground returns almost vanish. Make the collider visible and a grey mesh shows up in the camera. In what we tried, RTX offered no single "hide from camera but keep for shadows and sensors" switch — Kit's "Invisible to Primary Ray" property (primvars:hideForCamera) renders the mesh solid black, setting primvars:invisibleToPrimaryRays left it fully visible in real-time RTX, and pushing the mesh 4.5 cm inside the splats showed through the semi-transparent road. Untested candidates are listed in section 05. Demos 1 and 2 are structured to work around this.

02 Demo 1: Nova Carter + RTX LiDAR sweeping the city

Four steps. (1) Robot: reference /Isaac/Robots/NVIDIA/NovaCarter/nova_carter.usd through WheeledRobot (isaacsim.robot.experimental.wheeled_robots) and feed DifferentialController(wheel_radius=0.14, wheel_base=0.4132) into apply_wheel_actions. (2) Sensor: Lidar.create(path=..., config='HESAI_XT32_SD10', translations=[[0,0,0.55]]) under the chassis, then read LidarSensor(lidar, annotators=['generic-model-output']) every frame via parse_generic_model_output_data. By default the GMO x/y/z are azimuth [deg], elevation [deg], range [m] (elementsCoordsType = SPHERICAL), so convert to Cartesian. (3) Control: proportional control on the heading error to a 3 m look-ahead point plus yaw-rate damping (w[rad/s] = 1.2[1/s]·heading error[rad] − 0.25·yaw rate[rad/s]; not curvature-based pure pursuit). Commanded speed 1.0 m/s (sim time); measured from the chassis trace 0.97–1.0 m/s. All speeds below are those measured values. (4) Drawing: draw_points from isaacsim.util.debug_draw with world-frame points.

Traps (all measured)
  • The invisible collider is not seen by the lidar (above). This demo is shot in two passes: pass 1 makes a "sensor mesh" (same shape as the collider, render-only, no collision) visible and only records the world-frame cloud; pass 2 hides it, replays the identical drive and draws the recorded points. The sensor mesh has no physics, so both passes get identical physics input, and in practice the stop frame (1171) and the chassis coordinates sampled every 30 frames (2 decimals) matched between passes. PhysX reproducibility only holds for the same stage, dt and GPU/driver, though — re-check when anything changes.
  • What GMO x/y/z mean is decided by elementsCoordsType. It was SPHERICAL for our HESAI_XT32_SD10, but CARTESIAN configurations exist; read it and branch instead of assuming.
  • No motion deskew. All returns are transformed with one per-frame sensor pose, and Isaac's default also logs "MotionBVH for lidar model not enabled ... incorrect point cloud without motion effects", so the within-scan distortion a real unit shows is absent. For SLAM/registration tests, enable Motion BVH and deskew per point with the GMO timestamps.
  • Transforming GMO points with the sensor prim's world matrix put the ground exactly one mount height (0.55 m) too low. The translations given to Lidar.create do not seem to be part of that transform. Adding the mount offset puts the ground at its true height.
  • Do not overwrite the wheel DriveAPI yourself. Damping 1e5 etc. made the robot thrash on acceleration. Leaving it to WheeledRobot with the asset defaults is stable.
  • Caster shimmy. On the raw scan road the front casters start oscillating above ~1.3 m/s and the robot spins within a few hundred ms (1.5 s at 2.0 m/s, 2.5 s at 1.3 m/s). Stable at 1.0 m/s and below. We consider this a simulator-side effect of normal noise and contact generation on the raw scan road, not a reproduction of the real Nova Carter (no comparison with the real robot was made), so controller gains or stability tuned in this environment do not transfer with any guarantee. Faster tests need a smoothed road or caster damping.
  • Re-referencing the robot at the same path drops its body meshes. RemovePrim then add_reference without closing the stage left only the sensors rendering. Re-open the stage per take.
  • Ground hits look missing because the points sit at the same depth as the splat surface and lose the z-test. Lifting them 8 cm at draw time fixes it (apply the lift only in draw_points; keep recorded/saved clouds in raw world coordinates).

03 Demo 2: raw-mesh shadows on the splats (sun time-lapse)

As the Omniverse Particle Fields docs put it, "Particle fields will receive shadows from other scene geometry" — in 6.0 mesh shadows do land on the splats (the robot's shadow in Demo 1 is exactly that). But splat colour is baked and bypasses tonemapping (display-referred sRGB rendered as-is), so adding lights does not brighten anything and colour temperature does not change. The accurate 6.0 statement is: shadows yes, relighting no.

To cast building and lamp shadows you use the raw mesh as caster, but as noted the caster cannot be hidden from the camera. So this is a three-pass composite: (1) beauty = splats only, no sun; (2) A = raw mesh in a white material + sun, shadows on; (3) B = same as A with primvars:doNotCastShadows=True. final = beauty × (A/B) extracts the mesh-derived shadow mask alone. 72 sun angles × 3 passes took about 6 minutes. Turning off /rtx/shadows/enabled did not remove shadows from the B pass (measured) — use doNotCastShadows on the caster.

LimitsThe shadow mask is rendered from the raw mesh and multiplied onto the splats; the two surfaces differ by a median 6.8 cm on the driven road and up to a 95th-percentile 1.3 m in the far field (section 05), so the composited shadows are displaced by the same amount — fine for near road shadows, wrong against splat outlines for distant building shadows. It is also a fixed-camera offline composite, not physically based lighting: no indirect light, sky colour change or bounce, and A/B is numerically unstable in deep shadow (we normalise the ratio over 0.45–0.97, blur and floor it). Three passes per sun angle take about 5 s, so it cannot be applied online to a moving robot camera; a moving camera is untested. Received shadows are "blobby" by design and stack on the shadows baked into the scan, so overcast scans look most natural. Night scenes or lit neon are not possible in 6.0; that needs a relightable 3DGS on the training side.

04 Demo 3: occupancy grid + A* autonomous navigation

Map: isaacsim.asset.gen.omap (bundled). _omap.Generator(physx, stage_id) → update_settings(0.10, 1, 0, 0.5) → set_transform(origin, min, max) → generate2d() → get_buffer(); the 68 × 44 m Shibuya region at 10 cm cells takes seconds. Use a slab from ground + 15 cm to 75 cm so small road bumps are not treated as obstacles. Path: inflate by 0.42 m, 8-connected A*, line-of-sight pruning, then the same heading-error controller as Demo 1; goal tolerance 0.5 m. Nova Carter's footprint measured from the USD bounding box is ~0.71 × 0.48 m (circumscribed radius 0.428 m), so 0.42 m of inflation is slightly less than the circumscribed radius and adds no safety margin (10 cm cell quantisation on top); use ~0.5 m in tight spaces. The 0.75 m slab top is 5 cm below the ~0.8 m robot height including the lidar mast, so anything protruding only between 0.75 and 0.80 m is not mapped; we checked that no collider cells exist only in the 0.75–0.85 m band inside the driven area before accepting 0.75 m — raise it to ~0.85 m for general use.

Traps
  • The occupancy buffer columns came out mirrored in x. With our set_transform(origin, min, max), reshaping get_buffer() to (H, W) gave rows from YMIN but columns from XMAX (measured on Isaac Sim 6.0.1; whether this is omap's convention or our argument interpretation is not isolated). Against a ground-truth grid of collider surface cells in the same slab, the raw buffer gave IoU 0.08 and recall 16.8%; after a left-right flip IoU 0.62 and recall 100%. The map's occupied fraction is 15.2%, so the 100% recall is not an artefact of over-occupancy (IoU stays below 1 because omap fills building interiors while the ground truth is surface cells only). Without the flip the planner "avoids empty road and drives into obstacles" (our first goal landed on top of the police box). Always validate orientation against ground truth when the asset or frame changes.
  • A local import omni.physx makes omni a local variable and earlier omni.usd references raise UnboundLocalError. Use from omni.physx import get_physx_interface.
  • Kerbs (15 cm) in the collider are not obstacles with this slab. Carter's front casters cannot climb them, so if a route may mount the pavement, lower the slab bottom to 5 cm or add a height-difference cost.

On Nav2: the Isaac Sim 6.0 docs describe a ROS 2 Jazzy + Pixi workspace for Windows, but this article uses its own A* and no ROS. The grid exports to PNG via get_colored_byte_buffer, but a Nav2 map YAML also needs origin (world coordinates of the image's bottom-left corner + yaw), negate, occupied_thresh/free_thresh, and the PGM convention white = free, black = occupied, rows in descending Y. Given the x-flip above the conversion is not trivial, and we have not yet driven it under Nav2 — that is the next article.

05 What is still missing for a production simulator

"It ran" and "it is usable" are different things, so here is the honest list.

  • The camera and the LiDAR/collision see different surfaces (geometric gap). RGB comes from the splats and collision from the raw mesh. LiDAR and depth return whichever mesh is visible: only in the pass with the sensor mesh visible do they return the mesh surface (and then the camera shows the grey mesh) — RGB and range sensors cannot currently be taken simultaneously from their respective surfaces. How ParticleField contributes to the depth AOV is unverified. Measured on 300 k randomly sampled road/pavement splats (opacity > 0.7; nearest distance from splat centre to the collider triangle mesh) in the driven area, distance to the collider surface is median 6.8 cm, mean 7.7 cm, 95th percentile 15.4 cm (vertically the splats sit a median 7.2 cm below the mesh); over all outdoor splats within the ±40 m scan area and below 10 m height (same sampling and distance definition), 90% are within 20 cm and the 95th percentile is 1.3 m (far facades, signs, trees diverge). Any use that assumes RGB and range sensors are co-registered — RGB-LiDAR fusion training, depth supervision, calibration validation — inherits this systematic error.
  • No semantic / segmentation ground truth. The camera sees splats; class labels can only go on the collider mesh, and the two do not coincide, so a mesh-side mask cannot be used directly as instance/semantic seg or bbox GT (in the SDG test of the previous article, segmentation of the 3DGS came out as one colour for the whole street). RGB is available and depth/LiDAR only under the conditions above; segmentation needs a mesh-side mask plus a tolerance analysis of the gap, which we have not done.
  • Simultaneous RGB + LiDAR capture has an operational wall. The two-pass trick is for video; real use wants camera = 3DGS and lidar = mesh at once, and today the sensor mesh shows in the camera while the lidar captures. Untested candidates: (1) a fully transparent material on the sensor mesh, keeping geometry hits but nothing in the camera; (2) separate render products for camera and sensors with the mesh excluded from one; (3) alternate-frame visibility toggling (frame-level sync). purpose=guide is worth a try but guide is not rendered by default, so it will most likely vanish from the lidar too.
  • No motion distortion, default noise models. Motion BVH is off by default and points are transformed with one per-frame pose, so real skew is absent; intensity depends on the sensor mesh's uniform material. IMU, odometry and camera intrinsics noise models are unset, and no validation against a real-robot log at the same place has been done.
  • Ghosts and pedestrians. People caught in the scan remain as static obstacles; there are no dynamic agents. Adding animated pedestrians is not started.
  • ROS 2 bridge / Nav2 / TF / URDF. The Windows Pixi workspace is untested, and since no ROS is used there is no TF tree, robot description, /scan, /odom or sim time. Grid-to-Nav2-YAML works if you respect the corner origin, thresholds and row order, but it has not been driven.
  • Untested on Linux, low real-time factor. Verified only on Windows 11 + Isaac Sim 6.0.1; omap buffer layout and RTX LiDAR behaviour need re-checking on Linux. Capture ran at a real-time factor of ~0.05 (1.6 fps × 1/30 s sim per frame, Python drawing bound); HIL or human-in-the-loop use needs separate optimisation.
  • Relighting is shadows only. Time-of-day and weather are limited to shadows + compositing. No night, no rain.
  • Speed range. Carter on the raw road tops out at 1.0 m/s. High-speed or vehicle-scale tests belong to last article's PhysX Vehicle with suspension.

06 Summary and deliverables

  • Sensors: RTX LiDAR does not see invisible prims. Provide a visible sensor mesh; add the mount height when moving GMO points to world.
  • Shadows: ParticleField receives mesh shadows (official in 6.0). The caster cannot be hidden, so composite three passes. No relighting.
  • Map and path: bundled omap in seconds. Validate the x-mirrored buffer against ground truth. A* + pure pursuit reaches the goal.
  • Robot: let WheeledRobot + DifferentialController drive Nova Carter. Stay ≤ 1.0 m/s, reopen the stage per take.
  • Before you rely on it: the camera (splats) and LiDAR/collision (mesh) see different surfaces, a median 6.8 cm apart on the driven road. There is no segmentation GT, and no validation against real-robot logs, no Nav2 drive and no Linux run yet. It works for driving and sensor-capture workflow checks; it is not, as is, a basis for perception accuracy evaluation or sim-to-real claims.

When we hand over such an environment the deliverable is five items — the two-layer USD (3DGS reference + invisible collider) / the sensor mesh / the occupancy grid (PNG + origin & resolution) / spawn points and a driven-verified lane / the reproduction scripts from this article — plus the verified versions (Isaac Sim 6.0.1, GPU driver) with a spec sheet for frame, units and physics materials / an acceptance-test script with expected output (drive trace) / the licence scope of the scan data. The last item matters: the raw Shibuya scan contains pedestrians' faces and vehicle plates. Commercial delivery for AI training includes either masking or a contractual limit on use (e.g. internal validation only). The videos in this article use the raw data with no individual masking; the cuts were chosen so people appear only at low resolution several metres away — we will replace any cut on request.
