Setting Up a 3DGS Simulation
Environment for Isaac Sim

This is Nakamura from LOCAHUN 3D. This is a working guide to turning a LiDAR-fusion 3DGS scan into a simulation environment robots can drive under physics in NVIDIA Isaac Sim. The policy is radical simplicity: use the scanner's 3DGS and mesh untouched, and patch only the ground holes in the mesh. No clever deletion passes — we tried them, and destroying real content cost more than the noise they removed. A 16.5-million-gaussian scan of Tokyo's Shibuya scramble crossing serves as the running example.

What this article gives youThe inputs are the three standard outputs of a LiDAR-fusion scanner — the 3DGS, the scanner mesh, and the dense LiDAR point cloud (the procedure does not apply to camera-only 3DGS). With only ground-hole patching as post-processing you hold a two-layer environment — the visible 3DGS stacked on an invisible collider — ready for robot driving, collision, and sensor tests in Isaac Sim. Dependencies: Python, a point-cloud library, and Isaac Sim.

01 The big picture — why a collider at all

3DGS is unbeatable at looking right. But that is the result of optimising to match photographs, not of getting the shape correct — and used raw in simulation, two failures follow.

First, floaters: gaussians left where nothing exists land in the robot's camera as view noise. Under this article's policy we accept them rather than delete them — deletion passes keep destroying real content (see the optional section). Second, the real wall, holes in collision: 3DGS has no surfaces, so collision comes from the scanner mesh, whose boundary-edge holes run to 4.86% indoors and 9.01% in Shibuya. Put a robot on a hole and this happens.

The architecture that fixes both is two layers: a floater-cleaned 3DGS for appearance, an invisible watertight collider for physics. NVIDIA×Niantic's pipeline of the same shape constrains geometry with AI-estimated depth — but that is what you do when a 360° camera is all you have. Holding measured LiDAR depth, you need neither the estimator nor any retraining: the five steps below build the same thing (quantitative comparison against learned methods is in the benchmark section).

02 STEP 1: Load the 3DGS into Isaac Sim

Convert the 3DGS PLY to USD (ParticleField) and load it — the basic conversion workflow is covered in a separate article. For a working setup, defuse two traps first.

  • Align the coordinate frame. A Y-up 3DGS (e.g. from LCC2) converts to Z-up with positions mapped (x, z, -y) — a -90° rotation about X — and orientations left-multiplied by the same rotation's quaternion. When touching USD quaternion arrays from numpy, the memory layout is (x, y, z, w) — imaginary first; assume (w, x, y, z) and every gaussian's orientation breaks, turning the scene to noise.
  • Pin the renderer to RaytracedLighting before opening the stage. Path-tracing modes blow the self-emissive 3DGS out to white, and switching modes after a 10-million-gaussian scene is loaded is a reliable way to crash.
The unit trap when placing robotsReference assets authored in centimetres (Isaac's stock forklift among them) get an automatic 0.01 correction scale from Kit. Rebuild the xform with ClearXformOpOrder() and that correction disappears — enter a 370-metre forklift. After rebuilding, set scale 0.01 explicitly.

03 STEP 2: Patch only the ground holes in the mesh

Use the raw scanner mesh untouched as the collider. A voxel-healed watertight shell melts the geometry into wax, and patches cut from such a shell leave spiky fragments — both approaches lost. For thin poles and bollards alike, the raw mesh is the most accurate shape you have.

The only addition is ground patches: build a smooth ground field from the measured LiDAR surface (25 cm grid) and lay grid faces at field height only in cells where the raw mesh has no ground at all. Wall and facade holes never affect driving, so they stay. Shibuya: 514k raw + 114k patch = 628k triangles, zero leaks in the vertical ray test over the drivable area. The holes a robot could fall through are sealed; not a single other triangle changes.

04 STEP 3: Stack the two layers and drive

Assemble one USD: define a physicsScene on a Z-up stage, reference the 3DGS file as-is and stand it up with a -90° X rotation (no baked re-export needed), embed the collider as a Mesh with CollisionAPI (approximation=none), and call MakeInvisible() on it. That completes the two-layer environment: untouched 3DGS for appearance, raw mesh + ground patches for physics.

For the vehicle, use PhysX Vehicle (omni.physx.vehicle). The wizard API (VehicleData + create_vehicle) generates a 4-wheel car with sprung suspension and Ackermann steering in a few lines, driven by accelerator (0–1) and steer (-1–1). The suspension absorbs the raw scan road's small bumps, so the ride is smooth without smoothing the data. Two traps: set rootVehiclePath and rootSharedPath on VehicleData explicitly, and register your collider in the generated GroundSurfaceCollisionGroup.

05 Optional: cleanup passes, and their dangers

Deleting floaters and scanned-in pedestrians is technically possible — an earlier version of this article was built around those passes. After repeated measurement our conclusion is that every deletion pass lives next door to destroying real content. The accidents that actually happened:

  • Distance-to-surface floater deletion destroyed thin poles, signs and far-street content missing from the reference mesh (mesh-only reference deletes 6.80%; merging the raw LiDAR cloud into the reference drops it to 0.74% — the difference was all mistakes).
  • Rectangle-mask de-ghosting took out a street lamp standing next to a pedestrian, leaving its head floating in mid-air; silhouette masks plus LiDAR-detected pole protection were needed to make it safe.
  • Floating-fragment removal misclassified bollards — safety-critical collision geometry scanned as disconnected pieces — as debris and deleted them.

If you must clean up: (1) always merge the raw LiDAR cloud into the reference surface, (2) delete via silhouette masks with poles and bollards protected by LiDAR verticality detection, and (3) after every pass, render before/after from the same viewpoint and visually account for every object that disappeared. For collision and path-planning work, not deleting is the safest option.

06 Benchmark: relighting does NOT work (yet)

A 3DGS bakes in the light of its capture day. We tested whether post-processing can change the time of day, and record the negative result.

  • Scene lights do nothing. In Isaac Sim (ParticleField rendering), swinging a DistantLight from 8000 to 300 intensity and white to sunset colour changed the rendered pixels by no more than render noise (mean 2.7/255). 3DGS emits its own baked radiance; lights cannot illuminate it.
  • Shadow baking fails on quality. We ray-tested each gaussian toward the sun against the watertight collider and graded lit versus shadowed gaussians, at both 18° and 35° sun elevations. The new shadows stack on top of the shading already baked into the scan, reading as fading and dirt rather than evening light. Per-gaussian colour edits cannot avoid this double-shading in principle.
  • The only real route is inverse-rendering retraining along the lines of GS³, which cannot be bolted onto an existing scan. If you need time-of-day variants today, scan at multiple times of day.

07 Benchmark data and limits

The recipe was selected indoors (a meeting room), where ground truth is easy to measure. Healing takes the scanner mesh from 4.86% holes to 0%, with a median deviation from LiDAR of 1.1 cm, desk-leg occupancy of 29.7% (3DGS truth 27.2%; raw scanner mesh 18.6%), and 56/56 cubes at rest in the drop test. Drive a robot at a desk and it touches the edge and stops within ±5 mm — no clipping, no climbing.

What this meansA plain rule — do not delete or invent measured data — beat the learned priors. Niantic reached for a depth model because a 360° camera was all they had. Anyone holding measured LiDAR has a shorter, more certain route. The best answer to the same problem changes with the hardware in your hands.
Limits of this procedureThe numbers come from two scenes: the meeting room and Shibuya. No surface can be built where the LiDAR never looked, so keep spawns and routes inside the observed region when training. Outdoors adds two caveats. First, under the untouched policy, scanned-in pedestrians, parked vehicles and road shadow stains all remain (they act as real obstacles in collision work). Second, if you do clean up, follow the cautions in the optional section. Anything needing millimetre-accurate shape still deserves a CG model.

08 Summary

Turning a LiDAR-fusion 3DGS scan into an Isaac Sim environment fits in three lines.

  • STEP 1: reference the 3DGS untouched, stand it up with a -90° X rotation, and pin the renderer to RaytracedLighting before loading.
  • STEP 2: collider = untouched raw mesh + ground-hole patches only (628k triangles, zero drivable leaks in Shibuya). Wall holes stay.
  • STEP 3: stack the two layers in one USD, make the collider invisible, and drive a PhysX Vehicle with real suspension. No deletion, no retraining.

The basics of loading 3DGS into Isaac Sim are covered in a separate article.
