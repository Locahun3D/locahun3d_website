Direct from LCC2 to Blender: using 3DGS for VFX

A local prototype that loads the full scene without an intermediate PLY and exports footage and compositing passes.

I'm Kou from Locahun 3D. I'm prototyping a Blender add-on that reads LCC2 directly. Here is the workflow: load the full city, place a camera and CG, then export lighting, depth and masks for compositing.

Local experimental buildThis is not a publicly distributed add-on or an official XGRIDS product. Added lighting remains experimental; relighting has not been achieved. Use of the Normal pass, normal recovery and normal-quality improvements are outside the scope of this work.

01 Load LCC2 and frame the shot

Set the LCC2 source path and load the full scene with the dedicated renderer. No intermediate PLY is created. The source used for the full-data render tests is neither cropped nor decimated. Frame the shot with a Blender camera, then add CG objects and lights.

Use F12 for stills, or animate the camera and render an image sequence. Enable the additional compositing passes you need for F12 output. Compressed LCC2 storage and rendering memory are different: direct import alone does not guarantee lower memory use.

02 Added-light experiments — relighting not achieved

Sun, Point, Spot and Area lights are experimental tools for examining approximate brightness and shadow changes. CapturedColor holds captured color, AddedLighting holds the added-light contribution, and ShadowLoss holds the loss caused by shadows on that added lighting.

I rendered the scan alone, without added CG, using the same camera and exposure. This uses a separate viewer export from the earlier benchmark, retaining the full dataset without decimation. No Normal pass is exported.

Light position and color change the final image; warm light adds color to the road. Irregular road patterns remain, and this does not fully separate input-scan patterns from the lighting calculation. The scan responds to added lighting, but natural, production-usable relighting has not passed acceptance.

Captured shadows remainCapturedColor is not de-lit albedo. Adding a new light does not remove shadows already present in the scan. This does not recover materials or roughness, recompute indirect illumination, or reproduce Cycles' physical rendering.

03 Use depth and masks for separate adjustments

Depth is an opacity-weighted expected distance from the camera through overlapping splats. Treat it as an approximation for compositing, not exact surface geometry. Use Mist to drive distance-based fog or color adjustments.

GaussianMask separates the full scan; MeshMask separates added opaque meshes. Use Cryptomatte to select the full scan and added objects or instances. It does not automatically segment individual buildings within the scan.

04 Reload the EXR for compositing

Save color images and numerical data such as distances and identifiers in losslessly compressed 32-bit float EXR files. Do not apply display color transforms to numerical passes. The JPEGs in this article are explanatory previews, not compositing data.

I have tested lighting reconstruction and mask and Cryptomatte extraction from saved EXRs in Blender. Vector and MotionValid feed supported camera and mesh motion with stable correspondence into Vector Blur. I have not tested this in Nuke.

The ordinary F12 end-to-end checks that include the added Area accumulation use a small synthetic scene. They do not establish full-city image quality or performance.

05 Keep the scan, adjust the added elements

Load the full LCC2 scene in Blender and export added CG and compositing passes in one workflow. This prototype keeps the captured background while separating adjustments to added elements. It does not freely rebuild original lighting or missing geometry; real-city lighting quality remains under evaluation.
