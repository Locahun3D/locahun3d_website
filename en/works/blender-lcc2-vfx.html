Direct from LCC2 to Blender: using 3DGS for VFX

A local prototype that loads the full scene without an intermediate PLY and exports footage and compositing passes.

I am Kou from Locahun3D. I built a local Blender prototype that reads LCC2 directly, renders the full Shibuya scene as stills and footage, and sends depth, masks, lighting and motion information to the compositor. Here is how I use those passes to finish a scanned background together with CG added in Blender.

What this article coversThis is a local prototype tested in my own environment. It is not a publicly distributed addon or an official XGRIDS plugin. This article does not claim it is faster or more memory-efficient than other formats or products.

02 Working in Blender

First, point the prototype at the LCC2 source and load the full scene through its dedicated renderer. There is no intermediate PLY file. For this test, I retain the entire scan without cropping or decimation.

Next, frame the shot with a Blender camera and add the CG objects and lights the shot needs. Use F12 for a still, or animate the camera and render a sequence. Enable only the VFX passes needed by the compositor. These extra passes are F12 outputs, not additional passes continuously rendered in the viewport.

The imported 3DGS does not become a conventional editable mesh. Editing buildings as faces and vertices, or reconstructing unobserved backsides, is outside this workflow.

03 File size is not memory usage

Reading LCC2 directly removes the intermediate PLY export step. However, the size of the compressed source does not equal RAM or VRAM usage during rendering.

The renderer decodes the source and prepares CPU arrays and GPU-resident data. Depth sorting, output images, additional passes and shadow calculations also require memory. Storage size and working memory must be evaluated separately.

I did not measure peak RAM and VRAM across the full VFX workflow in this test. I therefore do not infer memory savings from the compressed file size.

04 Passes for compositing

Alongside the finished Combined image, the renderer exports separate information for specific compositing tasks. Treat color and brightness images separately from numeric data such as distance and object identity.

Depth and Mist: adjust by distance

Depth is the opacity-weighted expected camera distance of overlapping splats. ZFront gives the distance to the nearest splat center that meets the opacity condition, or to an added opaque mesh fragment. Mist supplies a distance-dependent compositing weight for effects such as haze or distant color adjustments.

Masks and Cryptomatte: separate the scan from added CG

GaussianMask isolates the scan as a whole; MeshMask isolates added opaque meshes. IndexOB gives an integer identity for the frontmost accepted target. Cryptomatte selects the entire scan dataset and added objects or instances.

I tested Cryptomatte by reopening the saved EXR in Blender and extracting the selected target. I have not tested it in the Nuke application.

Vector and MotionValid: finish moving shots

Feed Vector into Blender's Vector Blur to add blur from camera motion and mesh motion with stable correspondence. MotionValid marks where that correspondence is usable. It helps distinguish unsupported cases, including topology-changing deformation, from genuinely stationary geometry.

Keep compositing data in EXRUse lossless 32-bit float EXR for distances, masks and identity information, without applying display color transforms to the numeric data. The JPEGs in this article are explanatory previews, not numeric compositing assets.

05 What lighting can and cannot do

Add Sun, Point, Spot and Area lights to apply approximate lighting and shadows to the scan and added meshes. CapturedColor provides the captured color, Ambient the ambient-side contribution, and AddedLighting the added-light contribution. UnshadowedLighting and ShadowLoss separate the added lighting from its shadow loss.

CapturedColor retains illumination and shadows from the capture. It is not unlit albedo: adding a new light does not remove shadows already present in the scan. The prototype does not recover materials or roughness, recompute global illumination, or provide Cycles-equivalent physical rendering.

Treat these as signed scene-linear compositing contributions. Ambient plus AddedLighting plus Background reconstructs Combined; UnshadowedLighting minus ShadowLoss gives AddedLighting. I also verified these relationships through compositing from a saved EXR.

07 From a background to compositing material

The prototype connects direct full-scene LCC2 loading, Blender camera rendering and pass export for compositing. I use it to preserve the captured scene while adjusting added CG and visual effects separately. It does not make the original illumination or missing geometry freely reconstructable.
