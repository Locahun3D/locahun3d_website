Houdini 22 × ComfyUI — A VFX × AI Hybrid Workflow with Gaussian Splats from Shibuya

Senior VFX artist hectorVFX (based in Budapest, Hungary) shared the results of a new VFX × AI hybrid workflow experiment on X, built on Gaussian Splats (3DGS) of the Shibuya scramble crossing provided by Locahun 3D. The technique combines Houdini 22 with ComfyUI.

01 The trigger: Houdini 22's native Gaussian Splatting support

This workflow starts from Houdini 22, released in July 2026, which shipped native Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) support for the first time. Handling 3DGS no longer means relying on third-party plugins: splats can now be worked on with Houdini's ordinary nodes, just like any point cloud — animated with the standard deformers, or rendered directly in Karma. That shift, from "a background you can only look at" to a layer you can actually work on inside a 3D DCC, is what makes this hybrid workflow possible.

02 How the workflow flows

Based on the screen recording hectorVFX shared, the sequence comes down to four steps. The key idea: the background (Shibuya's 3DGS) and the subject (the car) are prepared through completely separate routes, and both are composited into a single Houdini scene while still being Gaussian Splats. Nothing is composited as a 2D image — the point clouds are merged in 3D space.

① Houdini 22: loading Shibuya's 3DGS natively

The Shibuya scramble crossing 3DGS data provided by Locahun 3D is loaded straight into Houdini 22. Running it through the dedicated Gaussian Splatting nodes turns the scan from "a point cloud you can only look at" into geometry with normals, ready to be combined with any other node.

② ComfyUI: generating the car as a 3DGS straight from an image

The white sports car isn't a photo composite — it's generated from scratch as a 3D Gaussian Splat inside ComfyUI. Rather than making a 2D image and converting it to 3D afterwards, the splat is written straight out of the diffusion model. Because it never becomes a mesh, it drops directly into the Houdini scene from step ①.

What is TripoSplat?It's a family of ComfyUI custom nodes that takes Tripo-style image/text-to-3D models and outputs Gaussian Splats instead of a mesh. Because the output is already a splat, it can merge natively into the Houdini 3DGS scene from step ① — that's the crux of this whole workflow.

③ Compositing in Houdini: two 3DGS assets become one scene

The car splat from step ② is loaded into Houdini and combined with the Shibuya scene from step ①. Matching its position, rotation and scale seats the car on the crossing, so that a real-world scan and an AI-generated asset end up as a single 3D scene.

④ Camera, lighting and the final render

A camera is set up in the composited scene with motion blur and depth of field, and the shot is rendered. A finishing grade — night-time neon reflections and wet asphalt — produces the final cut shown at the top of this article.
